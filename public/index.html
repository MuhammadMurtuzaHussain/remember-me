Open source · Telegram · Gemma on your machine

Plans get buried in family chats. Remember Me finds them again.

Someone you care for opts in once. Remember Me turns the plans in their chosen chats into short, source-backed memories, answers questions like “What am I doing tomorrow?” with the original message attached, and sends a calm morning briefing. A language model on the machine running the bot does the reading. No cloud AI service sees a word.

Try the sandboxRun it yourself

How it works

  1. Opt in.The person messages the bot and sends /optin. Until then, nothing is stored.
  2. Choose chats.They add the bot to a family group and send /watch. The bot tells the group, and anyone can send /notme to be left out and erased.
  3. Remember.Each text message becomes a short memory by a Gemma model run through Ollama. Answers always cite the original message.
  4. Stay in control./pause, /optout and /deletemydata work any time. A caregiver can pause and resume, never switch capture on.

      Privacy by construction
      
  
      
    
      Local model
      Memory extraction and answers use open-weight Gemma 4 through Ollama on the machine running the bot. There is no cloud AI key to leak and no third party in the loop.
      
    
      Real opt-in
      Capture is off for everyone until that person sends /optin. Group watching needs their consent too. There is no default recipient and no shared account.
      
    
      Bot, not a spy
      It runs as a normal Telegram bot, so it only sees groups it was added to, and every member can see it there. It never logs in as a person.
      
    
      Others in the chat
      The group is told when watching starts. /notme excludes a member and erases what was saved from them.
      
    
      Delete means delete
      /deletemydata confirm removes every row for that person and overwrites the freed pages. Data also expires after 90 days by default.
      
    
      Honest limits
      Telegram itself still sees group traffic, and a small local model can misread a message. So every answer cites its source, and briefings never pass through the model. The threat model lists what this does not protect against.
